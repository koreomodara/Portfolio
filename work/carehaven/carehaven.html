Overview

CareHaven is a HIPAA‑compliant Client Record Management platform I designed and built from the ground up for locally operated behavioral health group homes. Before this platform existed, these facilities managed everything on paper — handwritten shift notes, physical medication logs, and stacks of client records stored in filing cabinets throughout the facility.

I replaced that entire system with a centralized, secure digital platform that handles daily operations, clinical documentation, and compliance workflows — built specifically for non‑technical staff managing residents with developmental and behavioral health needs. It's currently in use by 4 behavioral health organizations managing 70+ residents.

The Problem

Small, locally run group homes operate under strict AHCCCS and HIPAA compliance requirements — but without the resources of larger healthcare institutions. Documentation practices varied widely across facilities: some managed everything on paper, others used disconnected digital tools with no centralized system tying it all together, and none had a comprehensive platform built for their specific workflow and compliance needs.

The real crisis came at audit time. When AHCCCS audits were scheduled, administrators had to scramble — digging through paper forms or exporting data from multiple disconnected systems, compiling everything manually, and hoping errors made weeks or months prior wouldn't surface. It was reactive, stressful, and posed genuine compliance risk.

  • Documentation errors that couldn't be caught or corrected in real time
  • No single source of truth for resident records, medication logs, or incident reports
  • Audit preparation taking days of frantic work instead of a few organized clicks
  • High cognitive load for staff already working demanding shifts
  • Physical storage burden for paper‑based facilities consuming space in already limited environments

My Role

I led end‑to‑end UI/UX design and front‑end development: defining information architecture for complex, sensitive datasets, designing user flows for high‑frequency tasks like medication logs and progress notes, creating interface patterns that balance speed with accuracy, and managing post‑launch UX improvements informed by real‑world usage.

How It Was Built

Early iterations were prototyped using Lovable AI to rapidly test concepts and get working builds in front of staff quickly. As the product matured and compliance requirements became more demanding, the infrastructure was migrated to a more robust stack — AWS for secure cloud infrastructure, Supabase for a compliant database layer with role‑based access control and audit logging, and GitHub for version control. This stack was chosen specifically to meet HIPAA requirements around data security, access control, and audit logging.

Design Process

1. Discovery & Requirements

Before building anything, I worked directly with group home administrators and caregivers to understand their day‑to‑day reality — what a typical shift looks like, where documentation actually happens, and what AHCCCS/HIPAA requirements they're held to. Staff were often documenting at the end of exhausting shifts, so cognitive load became a critical design constraint, and role‑based access was essential since caregivers, administrators, and supervisors all needed different levels of visibility.

2. Identifying Friction Points

Paper‑to‑Digital Transition — the biggest challenge wasn't building a digital tool, it was designing one that felt as natural as the paper forms staff already knew. I structured forms to mirror the mental model staff already had — same logical flow, same sections, just digitized and submitted with a single tap.

Form Abandonment — early usability testing revealed staff were abandoning documentation forms before submitting, creating dangerous gaps in resident records. The root cause: forms were too long, fields were unclear, and there was no confirmation state. I simplified form structure, added bulk action buttons ("Check All Yes / Check All No"), and added a clear confirmation state.

Audit Vulnerability — administrators had no way to quickly surface records during audits. I built centralized record management with search, filtering, and export functionality so any record could be surfaced in seconds rather than hours.

3. Design Decisions

I designed the interface iteratively and directly within Lovable, refining based on real staff feedback in the browser — a faster feedback loop given the team size and timeline. Decisions were guided by the realities of group home work: high readability for staff working quickly and tired, reduced visual noise on data‑dense screens like medication logs, fast repeatable actions for high‑frequency tasks, and high‑contrast, touch‑friendly targets for tablet use in the field.

4. User Feedback

After shipping the redesigned documentation flows, staff feedback was immediate, and the reduction in incomplete records was noticeable within the first week.

"It's so much faster now. I used to dread the end of shift notes and morning medications."
"Having everything in one place makes audits so much less stressful."

Constraints

  • Full access to the live system is restricted due to patient privacy and HIPAA compliance
  • This case study includes a test environment demonstration rather than live resident data
  • Every design decision was made within regulatory requirements — usability and compliance had to coexist at every step

Outcome & Impact

  • 4 behavioral health organizations actively using the platform
  • 70+ residents managed across multiple group home locations
  • 60% reduction in manual documentation, saving staff 2–3 hours of administrative work per day
