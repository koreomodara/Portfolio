Case Study

Disordered

An immersive mindfulness web experience designed to promote mental clarity and emotional regulation — an 8‑month capstone featured at ASU's end‑of‑year showcase and covered in ASU's newspaper.

Role

Lead UX/UI Designer, Project Manager, Research & Testing Lead

Tools

Figma, Visual Studio, Adobe After Effects, Notion

Timeline

8‑month capstone project

UX Design Web Accessibility
DS

Overview

Disordered is an immersive mindfulness web experience designed to promote mental clarity and emotional regulation. Through calming guided prompts, ambient soundscapes, and reactive visuals, users — particularly students and young adults — can engage in a moment of intentional stillness.

This project was an 8‑month endeavor created as part of my capstone. I served as the Lead UX/UI Designer and Project Manager, overseeing the design direction, user research, and coordination with developers.

The Challenge

Design a digital experience that encourages mindfulness and emotional regulation, feels intuitive, calming, and immersive, and balances form and function across devices.

Research & Discovery

Because Disordered is centered around emotional wellness, my first step was to understand how interface design affects users' mental states. I explored color psychology to guide emotional tone and reduce cognitive overload, typography and readability to support ease of use, interaction pacing to avoid overstimulation and encourage stillness, and existing mindfulness platforms to identify gaps in personalization and immersion.

I also consulted professionals in psychology and digital wellness to validate design assumptions. These insights shaped everything from layout and visual hierarchy to microcopy and animation timing.

Design Process

I started with low‑fidelity sketches to experiment with layout and flow. As the experience took shape, I moved into mid‑ and high‑fidelity wireframes using Figma. The experience needed to feel responsive, immersive, and calming without being too static or too chaotic.

  • A deep, cool‑toned color palette
  • Soft, sans‑serif typography
  • Minimalist iconography
  • Intentional white space
  • Smooth, meditative transitions

Showcase & Feedback

The project was exhibited during our senior capstone showcase and covered in ASU's newspaper, where Disordered received overwhelmingly positive feedback.

What Users Loved

  • The calming combination of videos, visuals, and prompts
  • Clear intention behind color, category design, and tone
  • Ease of navigation and thoughtful interface simplicity
  • Sound variety and aesthetics that enhanced immersion

Suggestions for Improvement

  • Adding more layered, immersive soundscapes
  • A voiceover toggle for written prompts to improve accessibility
  • Deeper ties to neuroscience for credibility
  • Mobile optimization to increase portability and usability
Professors and peers noted how the final version reflected "significant growth" from earlier iterations.

Outcome & Reflection

The final experience is a minimal, user‑centered interface that promotes mental rest through visuals and sound. It's a tool designed not to distract, but to guide users back to themselves.

Working on Disordered taught me how to lead a project end‑to‑end across design and development, translate abstract emotional goals into tangible UI decisions, and build a user experience rooted in research and empathy.
